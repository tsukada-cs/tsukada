Overview

Motivation

The inner core that governs a tropical cyclone’s intensity remains hard to observe.

A storm’s intensity, its wind distribution, and their rapid changes are governed by processes in the inner core—the eye and the eyewall that surrounds it. Yet opportunities to observe this region directly are limited, so estimates of intensity and structure still carry large uncertainties, and the underlying dynamics are not yet fully understood.

Approach

Making advanced use of satellite observations to diagnose and understand inner-core dynamics and structure.

From geostationary satellite imagery, which can capture a storm every few minutes, I have developed methods to measure winds inside the eye by tracking cloud motions and to estimate the radius of maximum wind. I also use synthetic aperture radar (SAR), which maps ocean surface winds at about 1-km resolution, to estimate intensity and wind structure more accurately. I aim to apply these insights both to understanding tropical cyclone dynamics and to improving operational analysis and forecasting.
